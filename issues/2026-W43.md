# Where agents get their feedback: blame, counterfactuals and self-improvement loops

**Rising in ML research, 2026-W43** · papers first posted Oct 5, 2026 to Oct 18, 2026 · [Read on Scholar Feed](https://www.scholarfeed.org/rising/2026-w43)

Several of this week's papers deal with where the training signal for agents comes from. ECHO assigns credit by tracing which decisions caused an error. ReSI and RSI-Forge build automated improvement loops for safety alignment and for research environments. RoboAware and OpenWAM learn from counterfactual outcomes, not only from successful demonstrations.

## The 10 papers

### 1. [Of Errors and Echoes: Responsibility-Aware Credit Assignment for Tool-Use Agents](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.16027)

Xingjian Diao, Tianyu Yang et al. · Computation & Language · Impact 15 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16027) · [Code](https://github.com/xid32/ECHO)

ECHO splits credit assignment into two steps: first identify which decisions are responsible for an error, then decide how to allocate credit. A frozen judge tells new errors apart from inherited ones and from corrections. The authors report the highest score on five of six tool-use benchmarks at both 4B and 8B scales.

### 2. [Open-MMUnlearning: Unifying Methods and Evaluation for MLLM Unlearning](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.10358)

Junkai Chen, Yuhao He et al. · Artificial Intelligence · Impact 16 · 5 GitHub stars · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10358) · [Code](https://github.com/JunkaiChen1/Open-MMunlearning)

An open-source framework for unlearning in multimodal LLMs that covers five benchmarks, eight MLLMs and twelve unlearning methods. In the authors' comparison, GD and MIP-Editor tie for the top overall score, and BLEU is the most reliable of the thirteen metrics they evaluate.

### 3. [IdeaAnchor: Teaching LLMs to Turn Literature into Research Ideas](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.08781)

Ziyu Chen, Yilun Zhao et al. · Computation & Language · Impact 14 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08781) · [Code](https://github.com/ziyuuc/IdeaAnchor)

Trains LLMs to generate research ideas from related papers, using structured specifications mined from published papers that describe how each input paper should be synthesized. The authors report consistent gains in ideation quality, with anchor-based training plus retrieval working best.

### 4. [Apertus 1.5: From Open Text Models to Open Multimodal Reasoners](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.15542)

Project Apertus, Yixuan Xu et al. · Computation & Language · Impact 14 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15542)

Extends the fully open Apertus models to image and audio understanding through continued pretraining on 4T additional tokens for the 8B model and 2T for the 70B, and makes the context window four times longer. The authors report that thinking mode lifts math reasoning by up to 20 points on the 8B model.

### 5. [ReSI: Recursive Safety Improvement toward Resistant and Resilient AI](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.12233)

Jingnan Zheng, Dongcheng Zhang et al. · Cryptography & Security · Impact 17 · 2 GitHub stars · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12233) · [Code](https://github.com/AI45Lab/ReSI)

Improves a model's safety alignment over repeated rounds. Each round runs automated red-teaming, develops training recipes, and accepts an update only if it passes a capability-retention gate. The authors report that the mean X-Teaming attack success rate across four models falls from 86.01% to 31.45%.

### 6. [OpenWAM: An Open Framework for Composable World-Action Models](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.07922)

Heng Yu, David D. Yuan et al. · Robotics · Impact 16 · 198 GitHub stars · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07922) · [Code](https://github.com/OpenWAM/OpenWAM)

An open framework for world-action models built on causal robot-video pretraining with configurable video-action interaction. The authors report robot-video training raises VTA success on LIBERO-Long from 68.4% to 97.8%, and counterfactual data lifts inverse dynamics to 84.0% success on held-out tasks.

### 7. [MemoWM: How World Models Change What Agents Need to Remember](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.10778)

Bingfan Zeng, Zhisheng Chen et al. · Machine Learning · Impact 15 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10778) · [Code](https://github.com/Feld-maxiu/MemoWM)

Uses a world model's shared predictions to decide what a long-term agent must store and what it can reconstruct later. Across five memory benchmarks, the authors report 42.42% average accuracy, 2.62 points above the strongest baseline, with 53.9% less experience-specific storage than MIRIX.

### 8. [RoboAware: Learning to Coordinate Embodied Skills from Counterfactual Outcomes](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.11480)

Bohan Zhou, Xingbei Chen et al. · Robotics · Impact 16 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11480)

Learns a coordinator that chooses which policy family should act in the current physical state, either modular skills or frozen end-to-end policies. It is trained on counterfactual branches replayed from the same state. The authors report a 77.0% overall success rate across 100 tasks.

### 9. [RSI-Forge: From Research Papers to Environments for Recursive Self-Improvement](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.09426)

Renxiong Wang, Darvin Yi et al. · Artificial Intelligence · Impact 15 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09426)

A multi-agent pipeline that turns published papers into executable environments with automated evaluators, producing 210 environments across 18 fields. The authors report that at least one model improves after its first attempt in 84% of 120 environments, and that models beat the reproduced paper methods in 68 of them.

### 10. [MeSD: Multi-Evidence Self-Distillation for VideoLLM](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.06342)

Weijie Zhu, Han Fang et al. · Computer Vision · Impact 14 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06342)

Self-distillation for VideoLLMs with three teachers conditioned on answer, temporal and spatial evidence, plus verification-guided optimization that treats successful and failed trajectories differently. The authors report consistent gains over RL and self-distillation baselines on video benchmarks.

## Also moving

- [ReCal: Calibrating Structured Pruning for On-Policy Distillation Recovery](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.11332): Adjusts calibration before structured pruning so on-policy distillation recovers more; reports gains of up to 16.7 points on AIME.
- [PHRBench: A Behavioral Evaluation of Post-Hallucination Reasoning in LLMs](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.10455): A benchmark of how 18 LLMs reason after hallucinated context; finds successful recovery is rare and tied to more belief updates.
- [BLiMPPI: Benchmark of Linguistic Minimal Pairs for Polarity Items](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.13607): Over 12,000 negative polarity item minimal pairs in 33 languages; multilingual models struggle most on lower-resource languages.
- [LadderEdit: Edit-Level Residual Compression for Memory-Efficient Lifelong Editing of LLMs](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.11160): Stores each lifelong-editing LoRA adapter as a low-rank sketch and raises its rank only when needed; reports 5.2x less memory.
- [EASE: Entropy-Adaptive Distribution Shaping for Evading AI-generated Text Detectors](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.09976): A training-free decoding method that uses predictive entropy to evade AI-generated text detectors, with negligible quality loss.
