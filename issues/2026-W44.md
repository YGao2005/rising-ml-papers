# Agents that check their own work before they act

**Rising in ML research, 2026-W44** · papers first posted Oct 12, 2026 to Oct 25, 2026 · [Read on Scholar Feed](https://www.scholarfeed.org/rising/2026-w44)

Several of this week's papers add a check between an agent and its output. DuoDiscover keeps testing its cheap proxy evaluators against the real objective. X-Ragent lets only valid measurements into a diagnosis. AutoAgent0 verifies driving trajectories before they run, and claimcheck back-translates formal specs to catch unfaithful ones. TiGanEval and Autonomous Science Bench measure where agents still fall short.

## The 10 papers

### 1. [X-Ragent: Tool-Augmented Reinforcement Learning for Evidence-Grounded Chest X-Ray Reasoning](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.16811)

Yi Wang, Zhenlong Yuan et al. · Computer Vision · Impact 13 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16811)

A reinforcement learning framework for chest X-ray reasoning that teaches a tool-using model when to seek extra evidence and keeps invalid measurements out of the diagnosis. The authors report the strongest results on three benchmarks, including an 8.6-point improvement on the held-out ChestAgentBench.

### 2. [DuoDiscover: Scaling Autonomous Discovery Beyond the Evaluation Bottleneck](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.17184)

Shubham Agarwal, Akshat Gupta et al. · Computation & Language · Impact 12 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17184)

A fleet of coding agents in which each agent pairs an optimization target with a fast proxy evaluator, and fidelity tests drop any proxy that drifts from the real objective. The authors report first place on 121 of 235 kernels on SOL-Exec and gains of 1.92x on vLLM and 1.38x on SQLite.

### 3. [Unravel: Recovering an Interactive 3D Scene from a Single Image](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.16146)

Hui Shan, Keyang Luo et al. · Computer Vision · Impact 12 · 44 GitHub stars · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16146) · [Code](https://github.com/SII-Hui/Unravel)

A training-free method that turns one image into a 3D scene of movable objects. It removes visible objects layer by layer, checks every edit, rebuilds the scene in reverse order and then settles it physically. The authors report more recovered objects and better placement than existing methods.

### 4. [Instructions Are Evidence, Not Objectives: Measuring Outcome Alignment of Agents](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.17757)

Kaiming Liu, Yuxuan Liu et al. · Artificial Intelligence · Impact 12 · 1 GitHub stars · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17757) · [Code](https://github.com/THUNLP-MT/TiGanEval)

Argues agents should treat instructions as evidence about the task, not the full objective. The authors find 56% of initial instructions in analyzed SWE-chat trajectories omit requirements, and on their TiGanEval benchmark eight frontier models lose 53 to 67 points of success rate on partial instructions.

### 5. [What Makes a Good Visual Representation for Spatial Understanding in MLLMs?](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.17844)

Xiaohu Huang, Yumin Guo et al. · Computer Vision · Impact 11 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17844)

Studies which visual representations help multimodal LLMs with spatial reasoning. The authors find a representation's 3D structure tracks how much it helps, and that explicit geometry needs distillation through a decoding head. Distilling both into one model, they report gains on 3D and 4D benchmarks.

### 6. [How Reliable Are System-One Decisions in Medicine? A Comparative Study of Jev, Jev-like Models, and LLMs](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.18529)

Yanrui Du, Danyang Zhao et al. · Computation & Language · Impact 11 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18529)

Compares Jev with other fast-decision models on 15 medical datasets totaling 8,742 instances. The authors report that Jev matches leading models on text-driven tasks but does worse on numerically driven ones, and has the lowest decision reversal rate. Most of the models handle abstention poorly.

### 7. [claimcheck: Measuring and Improving Specification Faithfulness](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.17358)

Martin Amster, One An et al. · Computation & Language · Impact 11 · 33 GitHub stars · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17358) · [Code](https://github.com/metareflection/claimcheck)

A benchmark of 3,234 rows in four formal languages, plus a tool that checks whether a formal spec matches its natural-language requirement. One model translates the spec back into English without seeing the requirement, and a second model judges the match. The authors report macro recall gains of 8.2 to 9.8 points.

### 8. [AutoAgent0: An Agentic Runtime for Safe Closed-Loop Driving](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.18866)

Jiageng Liu, Seth Z. Zhao et al. · Robotics · Impact 13 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18866)

A runtime layer for existing driving policies. It adds an independent detection module, a rule-based verifier that checks trajectories before they execute, and agentic recovery planning. The authors report better closed-loop robustness on Fail2Drive and NavSafe-infinity without retraining the underlying policies.

### 9. [PrivacyGuard: Source-Linked Sanitization and Locally Resolvable PII References for Cloud GUI Agents](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.16778)

Zian Yuan, Wei Li · Cryptography & Security · Impact 13 · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16778) · [Code](https://github.com/betterzian/PrivacyGuard)

Protects personal data that cloud GUI agents send to remote planners. It rewrites the full source of each sensitive value and links later appearances to locally resolvable references. The authors report 33.55%–82.60% less leakage than the closest prior system and cross-form continuity rising from 0.04 to 0.81.

### 10. [Autonomous Science Bench: Evaluating AI Agents for Autonomous Scientific Discovery](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.18449)

Yibo Wen, Han Liu · Artificial Intelligence · Impact 11 · 7 GitHub stars · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18449) · [Code](https://github.com/Yibo-Wen/as-bench)

A benchmark of six budgeted design-build-test-learn campaigns based on published biology, chemistry and materials experiments. Agents have to find good designs and also build a model that ranks unseen ones. In a pilot of 144 trials, the authors report a 67% success rate for the best configuration.

## Also moving

- [BRIDGE: Selective Boundary Repair with Dependency Graph Evidence in Multi-Agent Systems](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.17805): Repairs broken handoffs between agents using dependency graphs; reports up to 12.3 points higher task success on SILO-BENCH.
- [Proactive Physical AI through Continuous Observation and Timely Intervention](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.16967): Builds ProAgent, which watches the physical world continuously and decides when to step in, with fewer false triggers than its baseline.
- [Intent-Triggered Anchoring for Multi-Token Format Units in Parallel Decoding of Diffusion Language Models](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.16516): Training-free decoding rule that keeps multi-token format units intact in diffusion LMs; readable X-ray reports reach 100.0%.
- [PIGEONS: Incremental Long-Video Captioning with Persistent Identity Gallery and Evolving Overarching Narrative Summary](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.16066): Captions long videos clip by clip, using a fixed gallery of identities and a running summary to keep references to people consistent.
- [The Last Mile of Coding Agents for Long-Document Tasks: An Empirical Study on Clinical Trial Protocol Digitization](https://www.scholarfeed.org/paper/2610.18507): Tests coding agents on digitizing clinical protocols; finds they locate the evidence but fail at transcribing and structuring it.
